A fantasy text adventure from 1977, forgotten for decades, has resurfaced. It is called **Magic Caves**, it was written by physicist **John Burgess Trenholme**, and it was brought back to light on 19 November by **Jason Dyer**, who has spent years playing and documenting early text adventures on his blog The Renga in Blue.

## How Magic Caves was found

The source code was among the old files of a user who goes by gschmidl, who had downloaded it in 1999. The date leaves no doubt: one of the first lines of the BASIC listing reads "COPYRIGHT 1977 JOHN B. TRENHOLME".

Getting the program to run was not easy. As Dyer explains, the code is written for a very specific variant of the language, the business BASIC of MAI "Basic Four" computers, machines designed for offices rather than for gaming.

## What kind of game Magic Caves is

It is a cave exploration game driven by text commands, with treasures to collect and a maximum score of 2,800 points. There is also a wizard you can summon to teleport you, but the service costs 100 points. Kotaku, which picked up the discovery on 22 November, describes it as a missing link between Hunt the Wumpus and Zork.

## Who John Trenholme was

Trenholme was a physicist. According to Dyer's research, he had set up a low-temperature laboratory at IIT Kanpur, in India, and in 1972 moved to Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, in the United States. He died in 2020.

## Where to play Magic Caves today

Dyer has shared an archive with the original BASIC listing and a version that runs on Windows, linked from his article. Anyone with patience for late-1970s parsers can therefore try it right away.
