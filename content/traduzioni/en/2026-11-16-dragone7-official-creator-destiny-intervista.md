Tiziano is 35 and lives in Salerno, the same area that [Pirates: Black Sails of the Sea](/articoli/pirates-black-sails-of-the-sea-gioco-offline/) comes from, which we covered a few weeks ago. By day he is a developer and a full-time dad; then, Monday to Friday at 9pm, he goes live on Twitch and becomes **Dragone7**. For a while now he has also been an Official Creator for **Destiny**, meaning one of the creators that **Bungie** officially recognises for their content about its shooter. We asked him how he got there, starting from the beginning.

## Who is Dragone7, the Destiny 2 creator who plays "Ride or Die"

The name comes from a childhood passion for dragons, the huge ones that guard treasure in fantasy castles. 7 is his lucky number, and also the shirt number of Michael Vick, his favourite American football player.

In his Instagram bio he introduces himself as "The High-Speed Daredevil". He explained that the nickname comes from the "Ride or Die" philosophy he brings to almost every match: "I'm a player with a personality that goes from 0 to 100 in a millisecond". One moment he is calm and rational, the next, in his own words, "completely crazy and reckless". His team knows it, and so does the community. He adds that he always says what he thinks, even when it costs him a bit of goodwill.

## From the PC in his uncle's tobacconist's to Destiny 2's Day One

He saw his first video games on the computer in his uncle's tobacconist's shop: Quake, Unreal, Unreal Tournament. He describes himself as "raised on bread and Doom". Then came PlayStation and PSP, and the two titles he remembers most are The Legend of Dragoon and Spyro the Dragon. "Coincidence?", he writes to us. Two dragons out of two: I'd say not.

Destiny came into his life in 2017, on Day One of the second instalment. Until then he had only glimpsed it on the console of a friend who spoke highly of it. It was love at first sight, between the settings, the music, the gameplay and all the secrets Bungie hides in its worlds. He missed the first Destiny, which came out only on console, and he calls it "a big stain on my CV", hoping that "mamma Bungie" will give him a present sooner or later.

His class is the Hunter, the same as Cayde-6, the Hunter Vanguard who was the face of the game at the time. "You never forget your first love", even though he has betrayed it several times to learn the builds and abilities of the other classes.

## His best memory: a Contest raid completed with friends

When Bungie releases a new raid, that is, an endgame activity for six players that requires communication and carefully studied builds, for the first 24-48 hours it runs in Contest mode, at increased difficulty. Teams of Guardians compete to be the first to complete it. Dragone finished one together with some of his lifelong friends, and it is the moment he remembers most fondly. But he is keen to say that the best part, more than the completion, is the friends he has met along the way.

Destiny has had difficult seasons, and he doesn't hide it: "Bungie has tried again and again to make us lose the will to play". Asked what kept him there, he answers with a single word: passion. Then he lists the lore, the characters, the settings and the memories tied to each of them, the farming and the goals he still has to achieve.

## His first Twitch stream: 16 hours of The Witch Queen

His debut as a streamer was in February 2022, with a 16-hour stream in which he played the whole campaign of the just-released The Witch Queen. He barely slept. For him the most incredible thing was seeing his friends drop into the chat to say hello and experience with him a campaign he still considers memorable today.

He describes the gear he had back then like this: a pretty poor webcam, a claustrophobic desk and "a connection like a cave in Alaska". There were very few viewers, but he kept putting energy into what he enjoyed doing.

In 2023, after about a year, he came close to quitting, between work and some personal issues. He decided to take refuge in the good vibes of gaming, "and so... here we are, your honour!".

Today he streams Monday to Friday from 9pm. He organises activities for the community, for his clan and for people who arrive from the web and want to try Destiny's endgame for the first time, "raw and hard". The content that works best is guide-style: secrets, builds, mechanics, quests and hidden weapons.

How does he hold it all together? "The million-dollar question." Mornings are for work, afternoons are for the family, and in the evening "you put on your gaming suit and head down into the trenches!". He also admits that for now he doesn't really balance anything at all: he lets things sort themselves out, without taking space away from any part of his life.

## How you become a Destiny Official Creator

After what he calls "a year on fire" of livestreams, Dragone sent his application to Bungie. It was accepted a month later, thanks in part to IFreddo, whom Dragone already knew and considers one of the best Destiny creators we have in Italy. When the news arrived: "I cried, a lot, with joy". Then he called his friends to tell them everything.

For him the recognition means counting for something to the company that created his favourite game. And he points out that Destiny is considered a niche game, far from the fame of a Call of Duty or a FIFA, so content creators have to publish the right thing at the right time and work with other creators to keep a solid community going.

Since then, he says, his commitment to the game and the community has doubled. He started doing streams and collaborations with many Italian and international creators, and he went to Milan Games Week 2025 as a recognised creator, meeting friends and community members. There he also had his photo taken with IFreddo.

## Dragone7's next goals and his message to the community

For the next twelve months he wants to improve the quality of his streams and bring new content on Destiny, while waiting for the news that, in his view, will arrive towards the end of next year.

He asks the Italian community to close ranks around new players and those who want to improve, without pointless wars. In his opinion, a united environment gets noticed by publishers too: "A game with a healthy, cohesive fanbase gets better and longer support". And he signs off, like any self-respecting Guardian, with an "EYES UP, GUARDIANS!".
