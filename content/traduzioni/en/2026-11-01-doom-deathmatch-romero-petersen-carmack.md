**DOOM**'s **Deathmatch** invented a way of playing that we now take for granted. But in 1993, did the team at **id Software** really believe in it? A back-and-forth has broken out on X between three of its creators: **Sandy Petersen**, **John Romero** and **John Carmack**.

## What Sandy Petersen said about Deathmatch

It all started with Petersen, who recounted how the team at the time did not expect a large audience for the mode: in his view it was kept mainly because the developers enjoyed playing it. As an example he recalled the flop of *Command HQ* (1991), a multiplayer strategy game that sold around 40,000 copies.

According to Petersen, Deathmatch's success came later, thanks to the spread of local networks, especially at universities.

## John Romero's reply

Romero disagrees. He dug out a press release dated **1 January 1993**, eight months before Petersen joined id Software, in which multiplayer was already presented as a central feature of DOOM.

Petersen replied that he remembers a later discussion in which both of them had doubts about the mode's audience, adding that he could not know what had been decided before he arrived.

## John Carmack's figure on DOOM's first players

At that point Carmack stepped in: he trusts Romero's memory more and confirms that multiplayer was important to the team. He did, however, add an interesting figure. By his estimate, fewer than 10% of early buyers, perhaps even fewer than 1%, bought DOOM mainly for Deathmatch: hardly anyone had a network card or a null modem cable to connect two PCs.

Carmack also recalled that, years later, the decision to make Deathmatch the core of *Quake III: Arena* sparked a heated internal debate.

## Why this story still fascinates

DOOM is one of the most studied games of all time, yet its creators still tell different versions of events. This is not the first public disagreement between Romero and Petersen, and each time the community splits between period documents and personal memories. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who invented DOOM's Deathmatch?

The term and the mode were born at id Software during DOOM's development in 1993. John Romero is often credited with coining the name "Deathmatch".

### How did you play DOOM in multiplayer in 1993?

By connecting PCs over a local network (IPX), via modem or with a null modem cable between two computers.

### Can you still play DOOM in Deathmatch?

Yes: the DOOM + DOOM II version available on Steam and modern consoles includes online multiplayer.
