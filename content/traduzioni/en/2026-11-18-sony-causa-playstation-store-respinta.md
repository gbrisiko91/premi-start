**Sony** has won the British class action over PlayStation Store prices. On 17 November the **Competition Appeal Tribunal** (CAT), the UK tribunal that deals with competition cases, dismissed in full the claim brought by consumer rights campaigner **Alex Neill** on behalf of around 12.2 million PlayStation users.

## What the PlayStation Store lawsuit was asking for

According to the claim, Sony had abused a dominant position in the sale of digital games and add-on content on PlayStation, keeping prices higher than they should have been. Without that conduct, Neill argued, players would have paid around 20% less. The damages sought were around £1.49 billion, almost £1.97 billion with interest.

The trial had lasted about ten weeks, between March and May 2026. The claim had been given the go-ahead in 2023.

## Why the tribunal ruled in Sony's favour

The ruling, reached unanimously, starts from the definition of the market. Neill considered only games and DLC sold for PlayStation; the tribunal instead accepted Sony's argument that PlayStation competes with Xbox and Nintendo as a complete system, console plus games. Disc-based games and the fact that many players use more than one platform also weigh in the reasoning.

On this basis, the judges write, Neill did not prove that Sony was dominant "in the market she defined or in any other relevant market". The CAT also rejected the other allegations: the alternative scenario with prices 20% lower was not considered credible, and Sony's margins, assessed across the whole PlayStation system, were found to be neither excessive nor unfair.

## Will there be an appeal?

According to Push Square, the team behind the claim is considering its next steps. For now no appeal has been announced.

For those following Sony's finances, the ruling comes a few days after [the milestone of 100 million PS5 consoles sold](/articoli/ps5-100-milioni-console-vendute/). The full text of the decision is on the [Competition Appeal Tribunal website](https://www.catribunal.org.uk/judgments/15277722-alex-neill-class-representative-limited-v-sony-interactive-entertainment-3).
