A week before launch, the **GTA 6 map** has made its way online. On 12 November, photos of the printed map of **Leonida** included in the official Collector's Box, taken at a Selfridges store in the UK, started circulating on social media. **Rockstar Games** has not commented and has not shown the map officially, so it should be treated as a leak, albeit a very credible one. Grand Theft Auto VI launches on 19 November on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S.

## Where the leaked GTA 6 map comes from

According to GamesRadar and tbreak, the first photo was posted on X by a user who saw the Collector's Box on display in a Selfridges store in Manchester, while the sharpest image came from an Instagram account that was later deleted. The art style matches the promotional images of the box, which includes a double-sided souvenir poster: in all likelihood, that is exactly what this is.

There is also a rumour that a store employee leaked it, but there is no confirmation. The Collector's Box, called "Goodtime State: Vice City Collection", costs $399.99 in the United States and £349.99 in the UK, and it only contains merchandise: the game has to be bought separately.

## The Leonida locations revealed by the leak

The map is stylised and much less detailed than the one we will get in the game, but it is enough to give an idea of the scale. As well as Vice City, names such as Little Cuba, Port Gellhorn, Goose Key, Lummox County, Moreau, Ambrosia and Pelican Valley appear, in the Grassrivers area. According to some outlets' counts, there are five main counties, eleven islands and islets, and thirteen towns.

The general motorway layout is also visible, though not the full road network. And the most eagle-eyed fans have already spotted a UFO drawn above a quarry: it could be a nod to GTA 5's alien easter eggs, but that remains to be seen.

## GTA 6 nearly rejected in South Korea over nudity

Another curiosity emerged in the same hours. According to the minutes reported by PC Gamer and Gamereactor, the South Korean ratings board came close to refusing GTA 6 a rating, and without a rating the game could not have been sold in the country. The issue was out-of-context full-frontal nudity scenes.

There were three reviews, between September and October: the first ended in a tie, then on 16 October the game received an adults-only rating with eight votes in favour and one against. Those who played it through concluded that the scenes with nudity are not sexual and that the sex scenes do not show genitals.
