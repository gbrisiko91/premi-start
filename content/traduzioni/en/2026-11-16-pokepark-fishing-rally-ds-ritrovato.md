For twenty years **PokéPark Fishing Rally DS** was one of the hardest Pokémon games to track down, because it was designed to disappear. Now collector Daniel Quadri has shown IGN a working **Nintendo DS** cartridge that contains the complete game and can still transmit it to other consoles. The news came out on 13 November and Kotaku picked it up the following day.

## What PokéPark Fishing Rally DS was

In Japan it was called PokéPark Tsuri Taikai DS. It was a fishing minigame distributed in 2005 at PokéPark in Nagoya, a temporary Pokémon theme park. It was downloaded via the DS's Download Play, with no cartridge, and between May and September 2005 it moved from the park to a few Japanese Pokémon Centers. The sources don't fully agree on the exact dates.

You fished along a river flowing into the sea, casting the float and pressing A repeatedly when it was pulled underwater. Each Pokémon had a score based on size, level and rarity, and you could keep up to five catches. The species included Magikarp, Gyarados, Feebas, Dratini, Mantine and Relicanth. The best scores went onto a leaderboard that could be viewed at Pokémon Centers.

The game didn't last long, though: after 12 hours, or when the DS was switched off, it deleted itself. Only the scores already submitted remained. That's why surviving copies are extremely rare.

## How the playable cartridge was found

Quadri's cartridge sends the complete game to other DS consoles via Download Play, just like the park's stations did. According to Kotaku, it is the first copy confirmed as fully playable. Over the years demo versions and eBay listings had appeared, without confirmation. In July 2026, VGTimes recalls, a development cartridge put up for sale contained only the leaderboard software with visitors' scores.

Kotaku doesn't explain where the cartridge comes from. Quadri said it felt "surreal" to switch on the DS and go fishing again.

## The preservation controversy

Quadri sent the cartridge to PSA, the company that grades and seals collectibles in plastic cases. Many enthusiasts would have preferred a public dump of the game first, that is, a copy of the file saved for the archives, and criticised the decision. Quadri said that preserving a game like this matters a lot to him, but so far no dump has been made public.
