Italy's **2027 Budget Law** brings a **tax on LAN centres**. According to the draft budget bill that circulated on 31 October, every gaming station in public venues will pay €1,200 a year in entertainment tax, rising to €2,000 for large driving simulators. On top of that, an authorisation from the **Customs and Monopolies Agency (ADM)** will be required, with separate costs and a minimum of 20 stations per venue.

This is still a draft: the text may change during its passage through Parliament.

## How the LAN centre tax works

The measure is **Article 25** of the draft and amends Article 110 of the TULPS, the public security law that also regulates slot machines and amusement devices. It creates a new category for pure skill video games, with no cash winnings, played on PCs, consoles or simulators installed in dedicated venues. Playing at home is not affected.

The tax does not depend on actual takings. The State sets a flat-rate taxable base of €15,000 a year per station (€25,000 for the largest simulators) and applies an 8% rate to it. The result:

- **€1,200 a year** for each PC or console;
- **€2,000 a year** for each large simulator;
- for a venue with the minimum of 20 stations, **€24,000 a year** in tax alone.

## How much it costs to open a LAN centre under the new rules

On top of the tax come the costs of the ADM authorisation, which lasts two years: €200 per device, €1,000 per venue, €2,000 for each game offered and €10,000 for each online platform used. This last point will need clarifying: a typical venue has dozens of games and several launchers, and the bill could rise quickly.

The draft adds further obligations:

- each device must be uniquely identified and platforms must keep activity logs for at least five years;
- minors may enter, but only for games suitable for their age according to the **PEGI** rating;
- any form of betting is banned;
- for tournaments, ADM will set entry fees and maximum prizes, with a supervision levy of up to 8% of entry fees.

## Why the LAN centre tax is controversial

The main problem is the flat-rate calculation: a small provincial venue pays as much as an esports centre that is full every night. For many operators, who work on thin margins, €24,000 a year plus licences could mean closing down.

There are also doubts about the numbers. The technical report admits that there is no reliable data on how many LAN centres there are in Italy: for 2027 it assumes around 200 venues and 4,000 stations, with revenue of about €5.9 million. From 2028, however, it envisages around 2,300 venues, one for every municipality with more than 5,000 inhabitants, and takings of about €66 million. That is growth that is hard to imagine just as costs are going up.

Venues that are already open will have 60 days from entry into force to submit a declaration and keep operating provisionally, while paying the tax. ADM will have eight months to draw up the implementing rules. According to AGIMEG, the current transitional regime for these venues expires on 31 December 2026.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much tax will a LAN centre pay in 2027?

According to the draft 2027 Budget Law, €1,200 a year for each station and €2,000 for large simulators, plus the costs of the ADM authorisation. With the minimum of 20 stations, that comes to around €24,000 a year in tax alone.

### Does the tax also apply to people who play at home?

No. The measure only concerns commercial venues that make gaming stations available to the public.

### Is the LAN centre tax already law?

Not yet. It is contained in the draft 2027 budget bill, which must be approved by Parliament by the end of the year and can still be amended.
