This page explains what data is processed when you visit **{{NOME_SITO}}**, under Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR). This is a translation: if there is any discrepancy, the [Italian version](/privacy/) prevails.

## Who is the data controller

The data controller is **{{TITOLARE}}**. For any privacy question you can write to **{{EMAIL}}**.

## In short

- {{NOME_SITO}} **does not use cookies**, neither for profiling nor for statistics.
- We don't use traffic analytics or advertising.
- We don't ask you to register and we don't collect your name, email or other personal data.
- Our fonts are hosted on our own site: your visit is not shared with Google Fonts.

## Data collected while browsing

The site is hosted by **Netlify, Inc.**, which automatically logs some technical data about requests in order to run the service, such as IP address, browser type, the page requested and the time. This data is used to provide the site, keep it secure and fix technical problems, and is kept for the period set by Netlify. Netlify may also process data in the United States, with the safeguards required by the GDPR for transfers to non-EU countries. More information in [Netlify's privacy policy](https://www.netlify.com/privacy/).

The legal basis is our legitimate interest in making the site available securely (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

## The "Like" button

When you press "Like" on an article, to stop the same visitor from voting more than once we store an **anonymous fingerprint**: a code calculated from your IP address and the article, from which the IP address itself cannot be recovered. Alongside the fingerprint we only store the fact that you liked that article. The data is kept on Netlify's servers for as long as the article is published, and is deleted if you remove your "Like".

The legal basis is our legitimate interest in showing a reliable count. If you don't press the button, nothing is stored.

## Images and links to other sites

Some article images are official material loaded directly from their owners' servers (for example Valve for Steam, Nintendo or game publishers). To show them, your browser connects to those servers, which can therefore see your IP address, as happens with any website. Our site asks your browser not to tell them which page you came from.

Articles also contain links to external sources: when you open them, those sites' privacy policies apply.

## Your rights

You can ask at any time to access your data, correct or delete it, restrict its processing or object to it, by writing to **{{EMAIL}}** (Articles 15-22 GDPR). Please note that the "Like" fingerprint doesn't allow us to identify you: to delete it, simply remove your "Like" from the article.

If you believe your data is being processed unlawfully, you can lodge a complaint with the Italian data protection authority, the [Garante per la protezione dei dati personali](https://www.garanteprivacy.it), or with the authority in your own country.

## Personal features: search, saved articles and Checkpoint

Site search runs in your browser against a public article index; we do not send your search terms to an analytics service.

If you choose **Save article**, your browser keeps the saved article identifiers in its local storage (*localStorage*). If you explicitly enable **Checkpoint**, it also stores your opt-in and identifiers for articles already listed, so it can show what is new on a later visit. This information stays in your browser and is not sent to our servers or synchronized between devices. We do not use it for profiling or visit analytics.

You can remove articles from saved items, disable Checkpoint (which clears its history), or delete this site's stored data in your browser settings. If you clear the data or switch browser/device, saved items and history will no longer be available.

## Changes

If we change the way we process data, for example by adding statistics, advertising or a newsletter, we will update this page before doing so.
