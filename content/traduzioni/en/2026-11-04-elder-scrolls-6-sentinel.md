**The Elder Scrolls 6** finally has a name: it is called **The Elder Scrolls VI: Sentinel**. **Bethesda Game Studios** revealed it on 3 November, at the end of the video celebrating the studio's 40th anniversary. Eight years after the first teaser, fans now also know where they are heading: the province of **Hammerfell**.

## The Elder Scrolls 6 Sentinel: how the announcement came

The reveal came without press conferences or dedicated events. The video "Celebrating 40 Years of Bethesda Game Studios", narrated by **Todd Howard**, looks back over the studio's history and, in its final seconds, shows a short clip: no gameplay, just windblown sand uncovering an Elder Scroll, accompanied by a quote attributed to a priest in the service of the High Queen of Hammerfell.

Bethesda's message accompanying the video ends with a simple toast: "Here's to 40 more". The studio added that it was pleased to finally reveal the name and setting, but warned that it will be some time before it shows anything more.

The name is not a total surprise: at the end of October several leaks had already pointed to "Sentinel" as the subtitle and to a November announcement. Now, though, it is official.

## Why Sentinel means Hammerfell

For those not versed in the geography of Tamriel: **Sentinel** is one of the most important cities and kingdoms of Hammerfell, the land of the **Redguards**, west of Skyrim. It is considered the capital of the desert region of Alik'r, and it is no coincidence that the teaser shows nothing but sand dunes.

Hammerfell has been visited before in **The Elder Scrolls II: Daggerfall** (1996) and, more recently, in some areas of **The Elder Scrolls Online** such as the Alik'r Desert. According to PC Gamer, a quote in the footage places the story in the year 4E 206 of the Fourth Era, so at least five years after the events of **Skyrim**.

The desert is only part of the province: Hammerfell also has coastlines, mountains and green areas, so it is too early to say the game will be entirely "sandy".

## When does The Elder Scrolls 6 Sentinel come out

Here the news is less good. Bethesda has given neither a date nor a year, and has not officially confirmed the platforms. Bloomberg journalist **Jason Schreier** commented that the game is still "more than two years" away: this is his own estimate, however, not information from the publisher.

Since Bethesda is part of **Xbox**, it is reasonable to expect at least Xbox and PC versions, but the PlayStation question remains open: in recent years Microsoft has brought several of its games to PS5 as well.

## Why there's so much buzz

The Elder Scrolls VI was announced at **E3 2018** with a teaser just a few seconds long, and practically nothing had been seen since. Skyrim, released in November 2011, remains one of the best-selling and most replayed role-playing games of all time, in Italy too, where it lives on through remasters and mods.

The name was trending on X within hours, with fans split between those celebrating the return to the desert and those fearing another long wait. For Bethesda it is an important anniversary: in the same weeks there has also been talk of the other studios in the Xbox family, such as id Software, the home of DOOM, which ended up at the centre of the [dispute between Romero, Petersen and Carmack over the origins of deathmatch](/articoli/doom-deathmatch-romero-petersen-carmack/).
