The **Zelda Ocarina of Time remake** arrives on **Nintendo Switch 2** on Thursday 5 November 2026, worldwide. Two days before launch, **Nintendo** revealed who is behind the project, the first digital copies surfaced in Japan, and even **Google** prepared a tribute to the 1998 classic. Here is everything you need to know before picking up the ocarina again.

## When Zelda Ocarina of Time remake comes out and how much it costs

The date is confirmed by Nintendo's official page: **5 November 2026**, exclusively on Switch 2. It was announced on 8 September during the Nintendo Direct celebrating the series' 40th anniversary, presented by producer **Eiji Aonuma**, following the first teaser in June.

On the European Nintendo eShop, the price listed at the time of the reveal is **€59.99**. The physical version contains the full game on the cartridge (not just a download key) and, while stocks last, comes with a special embossed cover. Several retailers are already offering it at a discount.

## Who is developing the remake: the Tears of the Kingdom team

The remake is made in-house. In the first two parts of the *Ask the Developer* interview published by Nintendo, it emerges that the work is by **Nintendo EPD**, the internal group that has handled the mainline Zelda games for more than twenty years, putting to rest rumours of an external studio.

Direction is in the hands of **HokWai Ng**, making his debut as director after working on **Tears of the Kingdom**. He is joined by Aya Shida as art director, Hiroki Taguchi on programming and Masato Ohashi on music, while Aonuma and **Hidemaro Fujibayashi** are credited as producers. Parts three and four of the interview are expected any day now.

## What's new in Ocarina of Time on Switch 2

This is not a simple remaster: Nintendo talks about reimagined graphics, a revamped soundtrack and modernised gameplay. The main confirmed additions:

- **dash and jump** on a single button to clear obstacles and chasms;
- a **steed** to ride later in the adventure;
- time passes in every area, and new characters and enemies appear at night;
- the **Threads of Time** menu summarises the story and objectives;
- quick switching of weapons and items, motion-sensor aiming;
- **fully voiced cutscenes** and new dialogue;
- ocarina songs can be played with the buttons, by humming into the console's microphone or with a real ocarina;
- amiibo support to unlock masks.

There is something for number lovers too: the game takes up **24.5 GB** according to Nintendo's official listing, more than Breath of the Wild. The original **Nintendo 64** cartridge held just 32 MB. ## Google's Ocarina of Time easter egg

The remake has gone viral beyond the world of video games too. Searching for "zelda" on Google brings up the **Master Sword** on its pedestal in the Temple of Time: clicking it triggers the time-travel effect, and the results page reverts to how it looked in 1998, the year of the original. A fun way to remind us how much time has passed.
