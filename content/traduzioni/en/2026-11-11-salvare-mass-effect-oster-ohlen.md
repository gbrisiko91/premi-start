Someone is genuinely trying to **save Mass Effect**. **Trent Oster**, one of **BioWare**'s founders and now head of **Beamdog**, is working on a plan to bring **Mass Effect** and **Dragon Age** back to life after the studio's closure ordered by **Electronic Arts**. And the first name he mentioned to "get the band back together" was **James Ohlen**, director of **Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic**, who immediately said yes.

For now it is a project on paper, with no funding and no agreement with EA. But it is the first concrete response from the people who actually created those games to the fans' #SaveMassEffect campaign.

## Trent Oster's plan to save Mass Effect and Dragon Age

Oster discussed it in a series of posts on X starting on 4 November. The BioWare co-founder says he is building financial scenarios in an Excel spreadsheet, now on its thirtieth revision, and has a clear goal: a studio that makes the games the community loves BioWare for, without chasing the latest business-model trends.

The sticking point is money. Oster estimates that a triple-A Mass Effect would cost more than **$200 million**, although he believes an experienced, disciplined team could spend less. Above all, it would need the **rights to the series**, currently held by EA: without the intellectual property, he explained, they would only end up making money for the publisher.

## James Ohlen: "A chance to work on Dragon Age again is a dream"

Ohlen's reply came on 8 November, also on X. The designer, who spent 22 years at BioWare working on **Baldur's Gate**, **Neverwinter Nights**, KOTOR and **Dragon Age: Origins**, said that having the chance to work on Dragon Age again would be "a dream of mine".

That was all it took to set off fan excitement: Oster and Ohlen are two of the names tied to the Canadian studio's golden age, the era of role-playing games that defined a genre.

## Canada, the petition and what it would really take

The plan also has a political side. Oster wrote to Canadian MP **Don Davies**, who had called for BioWare's employees to be given first chance to buy the studio, and said he would welcome government support: Beamdog is based in Edmonton, just like BioWare. Meanwhile, the fans' petition has passed 210,000 signatures.

Many doubts remain, however. Beamdog is a small studio, known mainly for the Baldur's Gate Enhanced Editions, and an operation like this would require a major investor and a deal with EA, which has not commented. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why was BioWare shut down?

In October EA announced the studio's closure and the cancellation of the next Mass Effect; according to a Bloomberg report, the publisher preferred to focus on Star Wars Battlefront 3.

### Who owns the rights to Mass Effect?

The rights to Mass Effect and Dragon Age still belong to Electronic Arts, which has not said what it will do with the two series.

### Can Trent Oster really save Mass Effect?

Not for now: his is only a feasibility plan, which would require hundreds of millions of dollars and a deal with EA over the rights.
