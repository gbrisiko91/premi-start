The **Zelda Ocarina of Time remake reviews** are in and the verdict is almost unanimous: the return of the **Nintendo** classic on **Switch 2**, released on 5 November, debuted with an average of **96 on Metacritic**. It is the highest score of the whole of 2026, although it stays below the legendary 99 of the **Nintendo 64** original.

## Zelda Ocarina of Time remake's Metacritic score

When the embargo lifted on 4 November, the remake collected an average of 96 from the first 42-48 reviews, all positive and with no scores below 8. According to GamesRadar+, 25 outlets gave it a perfect score. With this result it beats the year's other heavyweights, from **Resident Evil Requiem** to **Phantom Blade Zero**, the Chinese game whose [record-breaking sales figures](/articoli/phantom-blade-zero-vendite/) we reported on.

The comparison with the past, however, is unforgiving for one reason only: the 1998 Ocarina of Time has a 99, the highest score ever recorded on the aggregator. The 2011 **3DS** remake stopped at 94.

In Japan, **Famitsu** gave it 37 out of 40 (three 9s and one 10): the same score as the 3DS version and three points below the 40 out of 40 of the original, the first perfect score in the magazine's history.

## What critics like about the Switch 2 remake

The critical chorus focuses on three aspects:

- **faithfulness**: structure, dungeons and secrets are the ones we remember, with an almost philological respect for the game from 25 years ago;
- **the graphics**: adjectives such as "gorgeous" and "breathtaking" come up in many reviews, and several outlets call it the best-looking game seen so far on Switch 2;
- **modernised controls**: running, jumping on a button, motion-sensor aiming and quick item switching make the adventure much smoother.

More than one reviewer also highlights the melancholy of the story and its theme of the passage of time, which decades later strikes those who were children in 1998 in a different way.

## The flaws: a single save slot and a jump that's "too easy"

The criticisms are few but recurring. The game offers **just one save slot**, a choice that did not go down well with those who share the console with their family. According to some, the new free camera takes atmosphere away from areas such as the ruined Castle Town, which in the original made use of unsettling fixed camera angles.

The most debated point among players is the **jump button**, absent in 1998: it makes some traps and some fights trivial and divides long-time fans. Nintendo explained that it wanted to make the game more pleasant to control, not simply different. The choice of Ganondorf's English voice is also causing debate, now that the cutscenes are fully voiced.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the Zelda Ocarina of Time remake's Metacritic score?

At launch it has an average of 96, the highest score among games released in 2026. The Nintendo 64 original has a 99.

### Is the Ocarina of Time remake the same as the original?

The structure and dungeons are very faithful, but the graphics, controls, voiced cutscenes and some additions such as jumping and the horse change the experience.

### Does the Ocarina of Time remake have more than one save slot?

No, the game allows only one save per user profile: it is the flaw critics mention most often.
