Modder **KOUZEX** has unveiled the **DualStation**, a kit that takes the motherboard of an original **Nintendo DS** and turns it into a living-room console, with HDMI output and wireless controllers. The project was shown in a YouTube video that follows the whole build, and the official website lists it as "coming soon": orders are not open yet and no price has been announced.

## How the Nintendo DS connects to the TV

The DualStation sends the image from both screens to a television over HDMI, at 60 Hz. The screens can be arranged in different ways, one above the other or side by side, and each game can have its own saved display profile.

There are several options for the touch screen: a USB mouse, Joy-Con in mouse mode, a controller's analogue stick or a Wacom Intuos graphics tablet for anyone who wants something closer to the stylus. According to Nintendo Life and TechEBlog, the console also has a built-in microphone, handy for the DS games that asked you to blow or speak.

The DualStation reads original cartridges for both the DS and the **Game Boy Advance**, is powered via USB-C and accepts Bluetooth controllers such as those for PlayStation, Xbox and Switch.

## What you need to build the DualStation

The kit contains the main board with HDMI and USB-C and some flex circuits to be soldered onto the DS. You need a working DS motherboard, while the shell, screens and buttons can be damaged: it is a way to give a second life to a console that would otherwise end up in a drawer. Assembly requires soldering tools and a certain amount of experience.
