**Activision** has apologised and will remove from **Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4** a piece of real footage linked to the **Itaewon** tragedy, the 2022 Halloween crowd crush in Seoul in which 159 people died and nearly 200 were injured. The scene will disappear with the update scheduled for **12 November**.

## What was in Modern Warfare 4's opening sequence

The footage appears in the game's opening titles, a montage of news reports depicting North Korea's invasion of South Korea, the heart of this instalment's plot. For a few moments, a rescuer is seen performing CPR on a person lying on the ground.

It was players who recognised the images: they had not been created for the game, but came from real footage of the night in Itaewon.

## The families' protests and Activision's apology

On 29 October the association of the victims' families and the Korea Game Users Association publicly called for the scene to be removed. The following day Activision responded with a statement to South Korean media, first reported by the website Inven.

In the message, the publisher points out that the game is a work of fiction, states that it never intended to evoke or trivialise the tragedy, and expresses its sympathy and apologies to the families and the victims. The removal has been confirmed for the 12 November update.

## Why the case caused controversy

This is not the first time the Call of Duty series has been criticised for using real material in its news montages, but in this case the wound is recent and the protest came directly from the families. Many players, especially in South Korea, are asking how such recognisable footage could have made it into the final version without being checked.

The game, developed by **Infinity Ward**, is available on PS5, Xbox Series X|S and PC. On the console front, this week also saw controversy over [The Duskbloods' delay to 2027](/articoli/the-duskbloods-rinvio-2027/), FromSoftware's Switch 2 exclusive.

## What remains to be clarified

Two questions remain open. The first is whether the 12 November update will replace the scene with other footage or cut it entirely. The second concerns the process: for a game of this scale, archive material usually goes through several checks, and the community is asking Activision to explain what went wrong.
