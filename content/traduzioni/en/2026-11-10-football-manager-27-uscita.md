**Football Manager 27** is available worldwide from today, 10 November. The new management game from **Sports Interactive** and **SEGA** launches simultaneously on PC, on **PS5** and **Xbox Series X|S**, and on smartphones via **Netflix**, and it is included in **Xbox Game Pass** from day one. For the English studio it is a delicate release: it needs to make people forget a previous edition that divided fans.

## Where to play Football Manager 27: PC, console and mobile

As announced by Sports Interactive on the [official Football Manager website](https://www.footballmanager.com/node/1106), three versions are available today:

- **Football Manager 27** for PC and Mac, on Steam, Epic Games Store and Microsoft Store, including the Xbox app for PC with Game Pass;
- **Football Manager 27 Console** for PS5 and Xbox Series X|S;
- **Football Manager 27 Mobile** for iOS and Android, included in a Netflix subscription.

On PC the list price in Italy is €59.99. Those who already have Game Pass can download it at no extra cost.

## What's new in Football Manager 27 compared with FM26

The heart of the new instalment is the **interface**, the most criticised aspect of FM26, the first edition built on the new engine after FM25 was cancelled. Sports Interactive promises fewer clicks, faster navigation with menus at the top and a cleaner full-screen view, as well as shortcuts, bookmarks and customisable panels.

Some features much requested by the community are also back:

- **cup draws** followed live;
- **transfer deadline day**, with last-minute deals;
- a revamped **news feed** and richer statistics, with heat maps and pass maps.

According to Multiplayer.it's review, there is also a brand-new mode, **Shuffle Draft**, developed with FIFAe: a kind of draft tournament for 2 to 32 participants, designed for shorter sessions.

## Football Manager 27 reviews: how the launch went

The first verdicts are positive. On Metacritic, at the time of release, the average stands at **82** from a small number of reviews, all favourable. Multiplayer.it gave it 7.5: the new tactical system, with more precise instructions for runs into the box, is convincing, while the match engine still shows sluggish defenders and the interface, though improved, remains slow for everyday tasks.

The overall verdict is that of a clear step forward from FM26, but not yet at the level of FM24, which many fans consider the last great instalment. How players react in the first few weeks will be decisive, after an edition that drew a lot of criticism on Steam. For SEGA, which has already been in the news in recent days over [the hacker attack on its ALL.Net services](/articoli/sega-all-net-attacco-hacker/), this is an important launch.
