**Xbox Helix**, **Microsoft**'s next platform, will be a console that can also launch PC games. So says Jason Ronald, Xbox's Vice President of Next Generation, in an interview published in the magazine celebrating the brand's 25th anniversary, which Xbox Wire put online on 15 November. In the same interview Ronald talks about neural rendering as a tool to improve graphics without pushing up costs.

## Xbox Helix and PC games

Ronald was keen to clarify the relationship between console and PC. According to the quote reported by Wccftech, Helix is "designed to be a console" that can also run the games on your PC, and not a PC that runs console games. The stated goal is to remove what he calls "arbitrary" barriers between the two platforms.

This isn't completely new: Microsoft had presented Helix in March 2026 as its next console capable of running Xbox and PC games, and at GDC Ronald announced that the first development kits will reach developers in 2027. We know the magazine's quotes through Wccftech and Multiplayer.it.

The practical details are still missing, though. Ronald didn't say which digital stores will be supported, so we don't know whether Steam or other PC stores will come to Helix.

## Neural rendering and price: what Ronald said

Ronald describes neural rendering as a new way of generating a frame without requiring more computing power or more memory. In practice, artificial intelligence models produce part of the final image. He didn't specify which techniques Microsoft will use: Wccftech speculates about solutions such as upscaling and frame generation, but that is the outlet's interpretation.

As reported by Multiplayer.it, Ronald admits that it is "definitely a difficult moment" for the industry: components cost more and memory has become more expensive. Microsoft, he says, must keep making technology accessible and affordable. Price, specifications and release date have not been announced; according to Multiplayer.it, official information about Helix will arrive next year.

Ronald also went out on a limb about the future: in his view, over the next three to five years we will see more innovation than in the last ten.

## Where to read the Xbox 25th anniversary magazine

The magazine was printed for the XBOX FanFest in Seattle and handed out to attendees. Those who weren't there can browse it for free on Xbox Wire, in a viewer embedded in the page. As well as the interview, it contains memories and behind-the-scenes stories about the history of Xbox and some news about upcoming games, such as Fable's capital, Bowerstone, on page 38.
