For a week, many **Japanese arcades** have been running at half capacity. On 1 November **Sega Fave**, the **Sega** group company that runs the arcade business, shut down its **ALL.Net** and **Aime** services after a **hack** on its servers. Today, 8 November, brought the first good news: some **Bandai Namco** games are coming back online on a trial basis, while there is still no date for Sega titles.

## What happened to the Sega ALL.Net servers

ALL.Net is the network that connects thousands of cabinets across Japan: it handles leaderboards, online matches, events and licences. Aime is the card system players use to save their progress. Both are used not only by Sega but also by other manufacturers, starting with Bandai Namco.

According to [Sega Fave's official statement](https://www.segafave.co.jp/news/261102_1/), an outside party gained unauthorised access to some servers. The services were suspended at 15:00 on 1 November, Japan time, to investigate and secure the systems. Back at the end of October the network had already been down for a few days to fix a vulnerability.

As for data, Sega Fave says that so far there is no evidence of any leak of customer or partner information. Credit card data, moreover, was not stored on the affected servers. The investigation, however, is not over.

## Which arcade games were hit by the attack

The outage has mainly hit games that live on online matches and tournaments. Among the most frequently mentioned Sega titles are **Initial D The Arcade**, which has cancelled several regional tournaments, and the rhythm games **CHUNITHM** and **maimai DX**, which are considering extending ongoing events and compensating subscribers.

The hardest-hit case, however, concerns Bandai Namco. Its games connected to ALL.Net can run offline for a maximum of **72 hours**, after which the licence expires. So, after three days of outage, **Taiko no Tatsujin** stopped booting altogether in several arcades, even in offline mode. Also affected were **Mobile Suit Gundam Extreme VS. 2 Infinite Boost**, **Wangan Midnight Maximum Tune 6RR PLUS**, **JoJo's Bizarre Adventure: Last Survivor** and **Mario Kart Arcade GP DX**.

The **Taito Station** chain described half-empty arcades and switched-off machines on social media, in a post that was later removed. Classics that don't need the network, on the other hand, keep working as usual.

## When ALL.Net and Aime come back online

From 8 November, as announced by the official ALL.Net account, six Bandai Namco titles are gradually restarting, with online play and card use: Taiko no Tatsujin, Gundam Extreme VS. 2 Infinite Boost, The Idolm@ster Tours, JoJo's Bizarre Adventure: Last Survivor, Wangan Midnight Maximum Tune 6RR PLUS and Gundam Arsenal Base.

For Sega games, however, maintenance continues with no reopening date. The next update is expected on **10 November** at 9:00 Japan time, 1:00 am in Italy.

For an arcade network, a week-long outage is a very long time: tournaments, seasonal events and subscriptions grind to a halt, and arcades lose takings right over the weekend. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Sega's ALL.Net?

It's the online network that connects arcade cabinets in Japan, used by Sega and other manufacturers such as Bandai Namco for online matches, leaderboards, events and licences.

### Was player data stolen?

According to Sega Fave, so far there is no evidence of customer or partner data leaks, and credit card data was not on the affected servers. The investigation is still ongoing.

### Does the outage also affect arcades in Italy?

No. ALL.Net and Aime are services designed for Japan; since the end of October, access had been restricted to Japan only.
