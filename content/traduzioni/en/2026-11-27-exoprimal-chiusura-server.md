**Capcom** announced on 25 November that it is shutting down **Exoprimal**, the team-based dinosaur shooter released in July 2023. The game will disappear from digital stores on 31 March 2027 and will stop working on 30 September 2027, when the servers are switched off.

The notice is on the game's official information site and opens with a line that leaves no room for doubt: "We regret to announce that service for Exoprimal will be ending".

## Exoprimal server shutdown dates

Capcom has split the shutdown into three phases. The original times are US West Coast times, converted here to Italian time:

- 25 November 2026, 2:00: the Premium tiers of the Survival Passes, from season 1 to 4, are no longer on sale. This phase has already taken effect.
- 31 March 2027, 3:00: the game, the DLC and all other content are removed from stores.
- 30 September 2027, 5:00: end of service. From that moment Exoprimal will no longer be playable.

The closure affects every version: PS5, PS4, Xbox Series X|S, Xbox One, Windows and Steam.

## Anyone who already bought the game can still download it

Between the removal from stores and the final shutdown, anyone who owns Exoprimal will still be able to re-download it and play it. That leaves roughly six months of matches after it leaves the digital shops.

The announcement says nothing about an offline mode, nor about refunds for purchased content. For any questions, Capcom points players to the game's dedicated contact form.

## What remains of Exoprimal after the shutdown

Exoprimal pitted two teams of five players against each other, each fighting waves of dinosaurs, and without servers there will be no way to start a match. It's a shame, because the core idea was one of the strangest to come out of Capcom in recent years.

When an online game closes, sometimes it's the fans who keep it alive with unofficial servers and mods. That happened, for example, with [Mass Effect 3's multiplayer, brought back to the Legendary Edition by a free mod](/articoli/mass-effect-3-multiplayer-mod/).
