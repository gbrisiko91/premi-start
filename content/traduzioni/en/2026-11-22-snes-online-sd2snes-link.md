The Super Nintendo can go online thanks to **sd2snes+ Link**, a hobbyist project that connects sd2snes and **FXPAK PRO** flash cartridges to the internet via a small ESP32 board. Its creator is developer **Luan Freitas** (ludufre), who works on mrehkopf's original sd2snes firmware; the project was highlighted on 21 November by Time Extension and is still in testing.

The announced features are plentiful: cloud save backups, RetroAchievements support, Satellaview simulation and, in beta, online multiplayer between consoles.

## What you need to play SNES online with sd2snes

The console alone is not enough. You need an sd2snes or FXPAK PRO cartridge updated to sd2snes+ firmware 3.0 or later, plus an ESP32-S3 N16R8 DevKitC-1 board to connect to the cartridge's USB port. According to Time Extension, these boards can be found in online shops for about $10.

The board is powered by a standard 5 V charger (the site recommends 1 A) and connects to your home Wi-Fi. No computer is needed: network setup is done directly from the TV.

## The features of sd2snes+ Link

According to the official page, the test version already includes cloud backup of save files, with up to three previous versions and 500 MB per account, and RetroAchievements support, for now without hardcore mode. There is also simulation of the Satellaview, the satellite service Nintendo offered in Japan for the Super Famicom, an internet radio and firmware updates.

In beta are XBand-style online multiplayer and an internet link for the Game Boy. A feature called Town is still in development. The cloud is currently limited to 800 users and there is no final release date: you can leave your contact details on the site to be notified.

## The SNES scene keeps going

Around the Super Nintendo there is a community that keeps churning out projects: it is the same one that brought [Castlevania: Symphony of the Night to the SNES with a free fan port](/articoli/castlevania-symphony-of-the-night-snes/). Anyone with an FXPAK PRO tucked away in a drawer has a good reason to dig it out.
