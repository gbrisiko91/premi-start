**itch.io** has declared war on low-quality **AI games**. The online store for independent video games announced on 3 November that it had deleted "a couple terabytes" of content mass-produced with artificial intelligence, and has extended across the whole site the filter that lets users hide projects made with generative AI. The move is making waves partly because, in the same days, **Valve** admitted that approvals on **Steam** are running late.

## What itch.io has done against AI-generated games

The announcement came directly from itch.io's official accounts, in a deliberately tongue-in-cheek tone. As well as the mass clean-up, the platform made three decisions:

- **anti-AI filter in searches**: the "no AI" filter, which previously only worked on some browse pages, now also applies to search results. Projects with AI-generated content can be excluded entirely or partially;
- **broader disclosure requirement**: for about two years, anyone selling assets for developers has had to say whether they used generative AI. The rule now extends to comics, books, board games and soundtracks;
- **spam guidelines**: projects created almost entirely by algorithms, with no real design work, or dozens of near-identical copies of the same product may disappear from search pages.

itch.io does not ban AI as such: the rules acknowledge that it can produce interesting results, but prohibit using it to fill the store with carbon-copy pages.

## Why itch.io deleted AI games right now

The reason lies in the numbers. According to the platform, over the past two months the number of new game pages created each day has **doubled**, while downloads and purchases have stayed flat. More pages, in other words, but not more players: the typical sign of spam. When a user asked what was behind this boom, itch.io replied by inviting them to guess, complete with a robot emoji.

Community reactions have been largely positive, although some developers are asking for more: a total ban on generative AI. That is a complicated path, because it would mean deciding where a game "made with AI" ends and where a game that only used it for a small part of development begins.

## Steam and AI games: Valve behind on approvals

The comparison with Steam is inevitable. Valve's store also asks developers to disclose their use of AI, but does not offer players a filter to exclude those games. During the latest **Steam Next Fest**, several independent developers complained of being swamped by mass-produced titles.

Also on 3 November, Valve published an update for developers admitting that it is "a bit behind" on reviews: the typical turnaround of 3–5 working days can stretch to **12 days**, and some studios say they have waited up to 20 days. Valve attributes the delay to having to review the same games several times and has published new checklists to help pass review at the first attempt. However, it did not link the problem to AI: that connection is being made by several outlets, not by the company.

It is not the only upheaval shaking the industry in recent weeks: just think of the rumours about the [closure of BioWare and the future of Mass Effect](/articoli/bioware-chiusa-battlefront-3/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I hide AI games on itch.io?

On browse pages, and now in search results too, there is a dedicated generative AI filter: turning it on excludes, wholly or partly, projects that have declared using it.

### Has itch.io banned games made with AI?

No. It has removed mass-produced spam and requires AI use to be disclosed, but projects that follow the rules remain on sale.

### Can you filter out AI-made games on Steam?

Not at the moment: Steam asks developers to disclose their use of generative AI, but does not offer players a filter to hide those games.
