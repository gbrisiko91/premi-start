A box of old floppy disks has given **Sega** fans back a piece of **Mega Drive** history. **Ken Rose**, a former programmer at the **Sega Technical Institute** (STI), the American studio that worked on Sonic in the '90s, has donated playable prototypes and source code of never-released games to the preservation group **The Hidden Palace**. Among them are **Jester**, **Segapede** and **Treasure Tails**, an adventure starring Tails.

## Which Mega Drive prototypes have been released

The material was presented by The Hidden Palace in its traditional Halloween special, dedicated this year to the STI's early years, from 1991 to 1994. Rose had kept the disks for more than thirty years and shared them after an interview with the group in December 2025.

The archive contains playable builds of:

- **Jester**, a prototype from February 1993;
- **Treasure Tails**, a build from April 1993;
- **Segapede**, a January 1994 version different from the one already known, with unseen levels;
- **B-Bomb**, an October 1992 prototype, further along in development than was thought;
- a unique version of **Kid Chameleon**.

That's not all: **source code** has also been released for **Treasure Tails**, **Segapede** and **Dark Empires**. Segapede's even contains a fragment of **Sonic Spinball**'s collision code and, thanks to the work of programmer vladikcomper, it has been fixed up so it can be compiled easily.

## Jester, Segapede and Treasure Tails: the STI's lost games

**Jester** was a platformer presented in 1994 as one of the Mega Drive's flagship titles: the protagonist was a little clay man who deformed his own body to get past traps and obstacles. Development stopped when its lead programmer, **Mark Cerny**, left Sega; he founded the STI and is now known as the architect of the PS4 and PS5. The prototype shows mechanics never seen in the magazines of the time, such as turning into a bouncing ball.

**Segapede** was a Sonic spin-off in which you controlled one of **Dr. Robotnik**'s mechanical creatures. Later renamed **Astropede**, it was cancelled after Rose left the studio. A version of it had already circulated a few years ago, but this build is earlier and different.

**Treasure Tails** is perhaps the biggest curiosity: an isometric puzzle-adventure starring Tails, which Sega wanted ready for Christmas 1993. Production times were too long and the team moved on to **Sonic Spinball**. Until now, only a short video of it existed.

## Why these finds matter for retro gaming

Every recovered prototype adds a piece to the history of an era in which internal documents were often lost or destroyed. Having the source code as well opens up another avenue: homebrew developers could study it or even try to finish these projects as fan games. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Treasure Tails?

An unreleased Mega Drive game, an isometric adventure starring Tails developed by the Sega Technical Institute in 1993 and cancelled in favour of Sonic Spinball.

### Where can you see the released Mega Drive prototypes?

On The Hidden Palace website, which has dedicated a page to each prototype in its Halloween 2026 special.

### What was the Sega Technical Institute?

Sega's American studio founded by Mark Cerny, which in the '90s worked on Sonic 2, Sonic 3, Sonic Spinball and other Mega Drive games.
