**Strauss Zelnick**, CEO of **Take-Two Interactive**, discussed video game clones made with artificial intelligence during an interview with IGN, reported by GamesRadar+ on 14 November. Answering a question about Anthropic's measures and possible initiatives from OpenAI, he argued that every company should protect both its own intellectual property and that of others. He did not accuse the two companies of copying any specific game.

## What Take-Two's CEO said about AI-made clones

Zelnick was asked whether he would like AI companies to stop users from recreating existing games. In his view, all companies should defend intellectual property, their own and other people's.

In his answer he cited Anthropic and OpenAI, already mentioned in the interviewer's question, as companies operating in the intellectual property business. He called it "ironic" to expect your own rights to be protected without respecting those of others. He did not point to any concrete cases of infringement attributable to the two companies, nor did he announce legal action against them.

## The Claude case and games recreated with AI

The two companies' public rules differ in how specific they are. In early November Anthropic added a rule to its assistant Claude to prevent it from being used to recreate a specific existing game, even if renamed. Addy Osmani, from Anthropic's technical team, explained that using Claude to infringe intellectual property is against the company's usage rules. Original games, genre games and games built with your own assets are still allowed.

[OpenAI's official usage policies](https://openai.com/policies/usage-policies/) already prohibit attempts to infringe other people's intellectual property rights. This general ban should not be confused with a public rule specific to recreating video games, worded like the one described for Claude: the GamesRadar+ article does not document an equivalent one for OpenAI. That does not mean copying an existing game is permitted.

The topic has been a hot one for weeks. Copycats of successful indie games have quickly appeared on Steam, and some developers have pulled demos or playtests for fear of being cloned. "Vibe coded" recreations of Mario Kart Wii, Banjo-Tooie and Zelda: Link's Awakening have also circulated online. Itch.io, meanwhile, has had a clean-up: we covered it in our piece on [the AI-made games removed from Itch.io](/articoli/itch-io-giochi-ia-eliminati/).

## Why Take-Two is talking about it now, ahead of GTA 6

GTA 6 comes out on 19 November on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S. In the same weeks, Zelnick described GTA as the most valuable intellectual property in entertainment and said the company has no intention of selling, following the acquisition of EA by a group of investors.

Zelnick does use AI, however. He has previously said that Take-Two has many internal projects under way with generative AI tools, but ruled out that these tools could create a game like GTA on their own.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Has Take-Two sued Anthropic or OpenAI?

In the interview Zelnick did not announce any lawsuits against Anthropic or OpenAI and did not point to a specific case of infringement attributed to the two companies.

### Can Claude still be used to make video games?

According to the rule described by Anthropic, Claude must not recreate a specific existing game. Original games and those developed with your own materials remain possible, subject to the other terms of use.
